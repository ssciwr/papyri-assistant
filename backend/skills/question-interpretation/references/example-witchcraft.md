# Example interpretation file: witchcraft and religion

This file meets every acceptance criterion. The question has three primary axes. Its key term is a modern category, and it divides time into two periods without saying where the boundary lies. Both are clarified with the user before the file is written; how they apply to the documents is left to the research.

---

# Question
In what way is witchcraft attested in the papyri, and how does it relate to religion in pre-Christian vs Christian times?

# Decisions
- Asked whether "attested" means documents that are themselves acts of witchcraft (spells, amulets, curses), or also documents that mention witchcraft or accuse someone of it. The user wants both.
- Asked what "witchcraft" should cover, since it is a modern term. The user: everything the papyri treat as magic, including spells, amulets, curses, love spells and divination.
- Asked where the boundary between pre-Christian and Christian times should lie. The user: not fixed in advance; it should come out of the documents.
- Final form: How is witchcraft attested in the papyri, as practice and as mentioned, and how does its relation to religion differ between pre-Christian and Christian times?

# Terms
- Witchcraft: magic as the papyri treat it, including spells, amulets, curses, love spells and divination (see Decisions). Which documents and ancient terms this covers is established by SQ1.
- Attested: both documents that are acts of witchcraft and documents that mention it (see Decisions); gathered as separate parts of SQ3.
- Religion: the religious practice of the same period as each document. Which practices these are is established by SQ5.
- Pre-Christian times, Christian times: the boundary is not fixed in advance (see Decisions). Where it lies, and whether a document belongs to a period by its date or by its content (e.g. a Christian invocation), is established by SQ2.

# Scope
- Period: all periods the papyri cover, divided into pre-Christian and Christian times; the boundary is established by SQ2.
- Region: open.
- Document types: open.
- Languages: open.
- Counts meant: not asked; any counts needed to compare the periods are of the documents in the database.

# Topics involved
| Axis | Role | What the question asks along this axis |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal | primary | How witchcraft and its relation to religion differ between pre-Christian and Christian times. |
| Material | auxiliary | Which documents attest witchcraft, and their type, date, language and place. |
| Relational | primary | How witchcraft relates to religion: which powers the texts invoke, and how these relate to the religious practice of the same period; who wrote, commissioned or used the texts. |
| Semantic | primary | How witchcraft is expressed: terms, formulae, invocations, and what the documents that mention it call it. |

# Sub-questions
- SQ1 (semantic, auxiliary): Which practices, terms and kinds of document in the papyri correspond to what the question calls witchcraft?
- SQ2 (temporal, auxiliary): Where does the boundary between pre-Christian and Christian times lie in the papyri, and how can a document be assigned to one of the periods?
- SQ3 (material, auxiliary): Which documents attest witchcraft, (a) as acts of witchcraft or (b) by mentioning it, and how are they distributed by type, date, language and place?
- SQ4 (semantic, primary): Which terms, formulae and invocations do the documents use?
- SQ5 (relational, primary): Which powers do the documents invoke, and how do these relate to the religious practice of the same period? Who wrote, commissioned or used the documents?
- SQ6 (temporal, primary): How do the answers to SQ4 and SQ5 differ between pre-Christian and Christian times?

# Synthesis plan
SQ1 and SQ2 establish the category and the periods on which everything else depends. SQ3 gives the documents and their distribution over the periods, against which the comparison in SQ6 is measured. SQ4 and SQ5 describe witchcraft and its relation to religion within each period, and SQ6 compares the periods. The answer combines SQ4 and SQ5 into a description of how witchcraft is attested, and SQ6 into an account of how its relation to religion changed.
